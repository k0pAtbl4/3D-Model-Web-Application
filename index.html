Type 930

1975 Porsche 911 Turbo

Porsche had already won with turbocharging on the track, and the 930 was the answer to whether the same trick would survive on a road car. It did, loudly.

Everything about it is wider than the 911 it grew out of. Flared arches to cover the rear tyres, a chin spoiler, and the whale tail that everyone remembers. Around 250 km/h flat out made it one of the fastest cars you could buy in 1975, and it came with leather, air conditioning and electric windows as standard, because Porsche wanted it sold as a grand tourer rather than a homologation special.

Under the bonnet, sort of

Three litres, one turbo, no intercooler

The bonnet you are looking at covers the luggage. The engine lives at the other end: a 2,994 cc air-cooled flat-six with a single KKK turbocharger and Bosch K-Jetronic injection. Compression dropped to 6.5:1 to live with the boost, which is why it feels flat until the turbo wakes up and then arrives all at once.

Porsche fitted a four-speed gearbox rather than the five-speed from the Carrera, because the torque would have eaten it. With that much shove per gear, four was enough.

Rear axle

All the weight sits over this wheel

The rear tyres are wider than the fronts and the arches were flared to cover them. Fuchs forged alloys, 7 inches wide at the front and 8 at the rear, on tyres that were about as much rubber as anyone offered on a road car at the time.

Suspension is semi-trailing arms and torsion bars, with the engine hanging behind the axle line. That layout gives astonishing traction out of a corner and a very short temper if you lift off mid-bend. The reputation the 930 earned for biting back came from exactly this part of the car.
